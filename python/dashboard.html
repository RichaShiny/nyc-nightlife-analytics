The story / Follow the evidence, not the premise

The original project asked whether nightlife districts experience an after-midnight rise. A decline can also be an informative result. Comparing raw totals across groups of unequal size cannot establish which districts are safer.

What would strengthen the answer? Historical venue coverage, visitor or footfall exposure, reporting-time sensitivity checks, and a credible comparison design around a policy change.

Analysis notes

Five hypotheses and how to interpret them
  1. After-midnight counts exceed early-evening counts: compare midnight–4 AM with 8 PM–midnight.
  2. Counts fall after 4 AM: compare 4–8 AM with midnight–4 AM; timing alone cannot establish causality.
  3. The midnight change differs with observed venue count: compare within-group percentage changes.
  4. Offense composition differs across groups: use proportions, with a consistent category mapping.
  5. The direction persists across 2019–2023: inspect yearly changes rather than unequal-length pooled eras.
Full-period estimates and descriptive uncertainty

These estimates use all years, boroughs and offenses, independent of the filters. Paired-month bootstrap, 2,000 draws, seed 9750. Month resampling does not account for serial correlation, venue-selection bias or exposure.

Data quality and denominators

Spatial joins use point-in-polygon only. Unmatched and ambiguous points are excluded and counted. Taxi polygons sharing an ID are dissolved. Calendar dates follow recorded occurrence start dates; no overnight business-date reassignment is used. Rates divide counts by selected zones and calendar days, not people. Daylight-saving exposure is not adjusted. Crime categories are analytical groupings, not legal classifications. The historical venue snapshot is applied to all years and does not establish historical venue presence.
