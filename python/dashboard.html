AUDIT / COMPLAINTSSource-to-output reconciliation

Every exclusion is accounted for

No unmatched point is forced into the nearest zone. A point must fall strictly within exactly one polygon; outside, boundary and ambiguous matches remain unassigned.

AUDIT / VENUESCoverage, not census

The coverage gap is material

226 venue points match; 18 do not. Only 84 of 259 zones have an observed venue. Zero-filled counts keep zones in the analysis, but do not establish the absence of nightlife.

1. Clean the complaint records

R keeps the first record for each CMPLNT_NUM, parses occurrence-start dates, and retains 2019–2023 with hours 0–23 and valid coordinates inside the broad NYC screening box. This removes duplicate IDs and invalid or out-of-scope records before spatial assignment. The source order determines which duplicate survives; this is not a “latest revision” reconciliation.

2. Join points to polygons—not to other points

Complaint and venue coordinates start in WGS84 (EPSG:4326). R projects both to EPSG:2263, matching the taxi polygons. st_make_valid() repairs zone geometry and polygons sharing a LocationID are dissolved. st_within() assigns a point only if exactly one zone contains it.

3. Keep the full zone registry

Venue counts are left-joined onto every NYC taxi zone. Missing counts become zero observed venues; groups are Low 0–4, Medium 5–14, High 15+. Complaint records and the zone lookup share LocationID. The dashboard uses all selected registry zones in its denominator, including zones with zero complaints.

4. Aggregate before sharing

The exported cube contains counts by year, zone, clock hour and offense group. Monthly group-by-window totals support uncertainty calculations. Individual complaint coordinates and venue listings are not embedded in the dashboard. Simplified WGS84 polygons are used only to draw the map.

# Independent spatial assignments in R
complaints$LocationID <- assign_zone(complaint_points)
venues$LocationID     <- assign_zone(venue_points)

# One count row per zone; preserve zero-observed zones
zone_lookup <- zones |>
  left_join(count(venues, LocationID), by = "LocationID")

# Many complaints : one zone attribute record
analysis <- complaints |>
  left_join(zone_lookup, by = "LocationID")

This schematic abbreviates the implementation. The pipeline exports zone attributes separately from the complaint cube; the dashboard associates them using a unique LocationID lookup. The monthly R aggregation joins the same cohort lookup directly. See R/prepare.R for exact filtering and assignment rules.

Counts divided by selected zones × calendar days give complaints per zone/day. The full period has 1,826 days. Window charts cover four clock hours; hourly charts cover one. No patron, resident, or footfall denominator is present. Recorded calendar dates are used without shifting post-midnight complaints to the prior evening. Daylight-saving exposure is not adjusted.

06 / THE TAKEAWAY

The evidence answers
a narrower question—well.

SUPPORTED

A descriptive time pattern

NOT IDENTIFIED

A nightlife-caused risk change

Reported counts, one high-group zone, and incomplete historical venue coverage cannot establish personal risk or a causal effect of closing time.

NEXT EVIDENCE

Activity and exposure

Historical venue inventories, patron or footfall counts, and a credible comparison around a policy change would make a stronger study.
